The problem

Music fandom is full of knowledge nobody can trust. The interview that explains the lyric, the ex behind the album, the academic paper that read it all correctly — scattered across forums, podcasts, and comment sections, asserted without evidence, and drowned in noise. Collecting it is the easy half. Knowing what matters is the product.

What it does

Two equal pillars. The engine: audio transcribed with speech recognition on NVIDIA GPUs, relationships extracted between songs, people, places, and documents, every claim scored against its own cited source by a natural-language-inference service. What enters the graph is not what someone asserted; it is what survived checking. Discovery: the layer that decides what deserves the pipeline at all. A discovery decision is treated as a claim in its own right — a source earns its place by measured, verified contribution, and noise dies by provenance failure rather than popularity. The fan never sifts the firehose.

Verification is never for sale, and confidence is never an opinion. Every confidence rating on the platform is the output of a model checking a claim against the source it cites — which is what separates a provenance graph from a fan wiki.

Who it is for

  • Fans of any artist, exploring the influences, histories, and relationships woven through the music they love.
  • Communities building theories together — submitting, corroborating, and challenging, with the platform tracking agreement against verified evidence.
  • Researchers and writers who need the story behind a song to come with its receipts.

Where this actually stands

This is an early-stage software company: pre-revenue, one operator, no employees, and no claims of traction. The engine runs today on the operator's own production cluster — sourcing, GPU transcription, claim verification, graph database, and web frontend, all deployed and monitored. The discovery layer is designed and is the next build. The platform is not yet publicly launched, and the founding corpus is still being built out. That is the honest position, stated here rather than smoothed over.

What it is not

  • Not a consultancy or a build-for-hire shop. This sells a platform; no services, staffing, or advisory work is offered.
  • Not a cloud, a GPU host, or a capacity provider. The cluster is the company's own development and inference environment; no compute is sold or offered to anyone.
  • Not a fan site for one artist. Taylor Swift's discography is the founding corpus and the muse — the product is the platform and the capability, for fans of any artist.
